Even as a little kid, I spent a lot of time on the computer. I loved video games, and at some point I realised: this is the future. Since then I've only wanted one thing – to be part of it.

## The first wave of rejections

In lower secondary school I applied for apprenticeships. I worked hard, wrote applications, prepared myself. In the end, it still wasn't enough.

That was a moment with two options: wait and hope – or change something. I chose the second one.

## A year on my own in England

I moved to England for a year. Completely on my own. My goal: perfect my English, become more independent and learn to stand on my own two feet.

By the end of the year I had the **Cambridge C1 Advanced** and the **ECDL** in my pocket. But even more important was what I learned about myself: that I can handle new situations, even when nobody is around to help.

## So close

I kept looking for an apprenticeship while I was in England. Towards the end of the year it got really tense: for an apprenticeship as an application developer in cybersecurity, I was one of the **final two** candidates.

I didn't get it. I was really disappointed – so close and still not enough.

## Keeping going

I allowed myself to be disappointed – and then I kept going. I kept working and kept applying.

And then it came: an offer from **Roche** for an apprenticeship in application development in Rotkreuz – an application developer after all, just where it was meant to be. Roche is one of the world's leading healthcare companies and known for excellent IT training. For me, that was the moment everything paid off.

## Not wasting a year

My apprenticeship starts in 2027. I didn't want to just let the year until then pass by. So now I'm attending the IT business school **WISS in Zurich** – as preparation, so I'm ready on day one.

## What I take away

Rejections feel like defeats. But every single one moved me forward: to England, to a C1 certificate, to more independence – and in the end, to Roche.

If you're getting one rejection after another right now: keep going.
